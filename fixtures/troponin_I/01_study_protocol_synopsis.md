# Study Protocol Synopsis

- **Protocol number:** BM-2026-01
- **Version:** 2.0
- **Version date:** 2026-08-15
- **Sponsor:** Sponsor Biomarker Review Board (SBMB)
- **Title:** A Prospective, Multicentre Biomarker Registry Study in Patients Presenting with New-Onset Chest Pain and Suspected Acute Myocardial Infarction
- **Type:** Prospective observational biomarker registry (no investigational product)

## 1. Background and rationale

Cardiac troponin testing is central to the diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction (AMI).
Conventional troponin assays lack the analytical sensitivity to detect the small rises seen
early after symptom onset. A **sensitive troponin I assay** (Troponin I Ultra) permits
measurement of troponin I at concentrations below the 99th-percentile upper reference limit
with acceptable imprecision, enabling earlier diagnosis.

The design of this registry is modelled on the published multicentre study of Keller et al.
(see §7 References), which established the diagnostic performance of the sensitive troponin I
assay when measured **on admission and 3 and 6 hours later**.

## 2. Objectives

- **Primary:** Determine the diagnostic accuracy of a sensitive troponin I assay for early
  diagnosis of AMI, at admission and serially at 3 h and 6 h.
- **Secondary:** Assess the association of an elevated troponin I with adverse outcome at 30 days.

## 3. Study design

- **Design:** prospective, consecutive-enrolment, multicentre.
- **Sites:** three hospital chest-pain units in Germany.
- **Enrolment window:** rolling, 2026-03-15 onward.
- **Index condition:** suspected acute myocardial infarction (see §5).
- **Biomarker of interest:** cardiac troponin I (TNNI3), measured with the sensitive assay
  described in `03_assay_instructions_for_use.md`.

## 4. Sampling schedule

Blood is drawn at three protocol-defined timepoints after hospital admission:

- **Admission (timepoint 1)** — baseline sample
- **3 hours after admission (timepoint 2)**
- **6 hours after admission (timepoint 3)**

The **rise or fall across serial samples is part of the finding**; a single admission value
cannot distinguish acute release from chronic elevation.

## 5. Study population

**Inclusion (key):**
- Adults presenting to a chest-pain unit with new-onset chest pain
- High pretest probability of acute myocardial infarction
- Written informed consent

**Exclusion (key):**
- Age < 18 years
- Haemodialysis at presentation
- Life expectancy < 6 months from a non-cardiac cause

The final discharge diagnosis is adjudicated by two independent cardiologists, unaware of the
sensitive troponin I results, with a third referee for disagreement.

## 6. Endpoints

- **Primary endpoint:** diagnosis of AMI (reference/adjudicated).
- **Secondary endpoint:** adverse outcome (death, MI, or revascularisation) at 30 days.

## 7. References

- **Keller T, Zeller T, Peetz D, et al.** Sensitive troponin I assay in early diagnosis of
  acute myocardial infarction. *N Engl J Med.* 2009;361(9):868–877.
- Universal definition of myocardial infarction (as applied at the time of the study).
